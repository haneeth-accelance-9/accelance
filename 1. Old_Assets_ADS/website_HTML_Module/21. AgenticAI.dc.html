Definition

What "agentic AI" means at Accelance

An agentic system doesn't just recommend — it acts, within limits you set. At Accelance, every action an agent takes is tagged with one of three governance states before it executes: autonomous, review-required, or mandatory approval. This is the definition we build to, and the one we're happy to be held to.

Three states. Every action carries one.

Autonomous · green

Low-risk actions the agent completes without waiting on review.

Review required · amber

The agent proposes; a person approves before anything executes.

Mandatory approval · red

High-impact actions that always require an explicit sign-off, with no override.

What this looks like

An action, before it happens

This is the actual component every Accelance agent renders before touching your system of record — never a silent toast, never an auto-applied change. Approve it, reject it, or open the audit trail to see why it was proposed.

Questions we get asked in every evaluation

What happens if the agent is wrong?

Nothing executes without the appropriate approval tier being satisfied first. Mandatory-approval actions cannot bypass a human decision, by design.

Can we change what's autonomous vs. review-required?

Yes — the tiers are configured to your risk tolerance, not fixed by us.

Does this replace our existing automation (RPA)?

No. Rules-based automation still handles the deterministic work. Agentic AI handles the judgment-adjacent work that rules can't.
